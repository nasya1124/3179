STATE AND TERRITORY PATTERNS

Where the forests grow

Australia’s native forest area varies across its states.
Queensland has the largest reported area, at 51,750
thousand hectares. The Northern Territory and Western
Australia follow. New South Wales reports a similar
total. The map locates these totals; bars support
direct comparison. These figures show total area, not
each state’s forest share.

Forest area: ABARES, Indicator 1.1a.i (2023) . Map boundaries: ABS, ASGS Edition 4 digital boundary files (2026) .

FOREST COMPOSITION

What they are made of

Australia’s native forests include many tree types and species. Eucalypt forest dominates reported area, while smaller forest types occupy distinct environments. Herbarium records show where three selected native tree species were collected. These records describe documented specimens, not tree abundance.

THE LARGEST SHARE

77%

of Australia’s native forest area is eucalypt forest.

Acacia is next largest, covering 10.9 million hectares.

THREE SPECIES · RECORDED LOCATIONS

Eucalyptus regnans records cluster in the southeast.

Araucaria bidwillii records appear mainly in Queensland.

Adansonia gregorii records cluster in the northwest.

These are herbarium records, not estimates of tree populations.
